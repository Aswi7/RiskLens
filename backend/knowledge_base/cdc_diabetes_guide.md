# CDC Diabetes & Prediabetes Prevention Guide
Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
Topic: Diabetes
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/prevention/index.html

## Understanding Prediabetes and Diabetes Risk
Prediabetes is a serious health condition where blood sugar levels are higher than normal, but not high enough yet to be diagnosed as type 2 diabetes. Approximately 98 million American adults—more than 1 in 3—have prediabetes. Of those with prediabetes, more than 80% don't know they have it. Prediabetes increases your risk of developing type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and stroke.

## Key Risk Factors for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes
- Being overweight or obese (BMI of 25 or higher)
- Being 45 years of age or older
- Having a parent or sibling with type 2 diabetes
- Being physically active less than 3 times a week
- Having a history of gestational diabetes during pregnancy
- Having high blood pressure or high cholesterol levels

## Dietary Guidance and Foods to Avoid
A healthy diet plan for managing blood sugar and prediabetes includes:
- Foods to Limit or Avoid: Sugar-sweetened beverages (soda, fruit juices, sweet tea), refined carbohydrates (white bread, white rice, pastries, sugary cereals), processed meats (bacon, hot dogs, sausage), and foods high in saturated or trans fats.
- Recommended Foods: Non-starchy vegetables (spinach, broccoli, peppers, tomatoes), high-fiber foods (beans, lentils, whole grains like oats and quinoa), lean proteins (chicken, turkey, fish, tofu), and healthy unsaturated fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil).
- Portion Control: Using the Diabetes Plate Method — fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables, one-quarter with lean protein, and one-quarter with high-fiber carbohydrates.

## Physical Activity and Exercise Standards
Regular physical activity helps your body use insulin more effectively:
- Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week (such as 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week of brisk walking, cycling, or swimming).
- Include muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week that work all major muscle groups.
- A weight loss of just 5% to 7% of your body weight combined with regular exercise can reduce the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by up to 58%.

## Blood Glucose Levels and Biomarkers
- Normal Fasting Blood Glucose: Less than 100 mg/dL.
- Prediabetes Fasting Glucose: 100 to 125 mg/dL.
- Diabetes Fasting Glucose: 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate tests.
- HbA1c Levels: Normal is below 5.7%, prediabetes is 5.7% to 6.4%, and diabetes is 6.5% or higher.
